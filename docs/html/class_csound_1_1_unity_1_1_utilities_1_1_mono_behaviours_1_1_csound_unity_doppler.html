Detailed Description

Writes the geometry between this object and the AudioListener into Csound channels, so an orchestra can produce its own Doppler shift and its own propagation delay.

Unity's own dopplerLevel cannot do it: it shifts a pitch by resampling an AudioClip, and CsoundUnity writes its audio in the filter callback, where there is no clip. Measured with the Doppler scene of the Diagnostics sample, a CsoundUnity and its children hold their pitch exactly while a plain AudioSource beside them sweeps. Everything else Unity does with position still arrives, distance attenuation and panning included.

On a CsoundUnityChild it writes to the instance that child reads, because that is where the sound is made. Every child is somewhere else, so each one needs its own channel names and its own delay line in that orchestra: one shift for all of them would be the shift of none of them.

Three numbers go out, each optional: the distance, the closing speed, and the ratio to multiply a frequency by. The distance alone is enough for the physical way of doing it, a delay line whose tap follows distance / speedOfSound: a moving tap is a Doppler shift, it is right however the two sides move, and it delays the sound by its travel time into the bargain. The ratio is the shortcut for an orchestra that owns its frequency.

instr 1
aSig poscil 0.3, 330
kDelay chnget "delay"
kSm1 portk kDelay, 0.02
kSmooth portk kSm1, 0.02
aDel interp limit:k(kSmooth, 0.001, 0.99)
aDum delayr 1
aOut deltapx aDel, 32
delayw aSig
outs aOut, aOut
endin
